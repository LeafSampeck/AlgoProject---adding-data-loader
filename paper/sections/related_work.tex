\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Mid-Price Movement Prediction from Limit Order Books}
Early work on limit order book (LOB) prediction relied on hand-crafted
quantities such as order-flow imbalance, which has a well-documented
short-horizon relationship with price changes~\cite{cont2014price}.
The FI-2010 benchmark~\cite{ntakaris2018benchmark} made LOB forecasting a
standard machine-learning task: it provides ten days of normalised
ten-level order book data for five Finnish stocks, together with
Up/Stationary/Down mid-price labels at horizons
$k \in \{10, 20, 30, 50, 100\}$ events. The original paper reports
ridge-regression and single-hidden-layer network baselines and recommends a
chronological, anchored day-by-day evaluation protocol.

Deep learning quickly became the dominant approach on this benchmark.
Tsantekidis \emph{et al.}~\cite{tsantekidis2017cnn} showed that a
convolutional network applied to a window of the most recent 100 LOB
snapshots outperforms SVM and MLP baselines, establishing the
$100 \times 40$ input format we also adopt. Sirignano and
Cont~\cite{sirignano2019universal} trained recurrent networks on a large
cross-section of US equities and found that the learned relationship between
order-book state and price moves is largely universal across stocks.
DeepLOB~\cite{zhang2019deeplob} combines convolutional layers that
aggregate prices and volumes across levels, an Inception module that mixes
several temporal receptive fields, and an LSTM that summarises the sequence;
it remains one of the most widely cited LOB models.

\subsection{Attention for LOB Data}
Attention mechanisms let a model weight the most informative time steps in
the window instead of compressing the history into a single recurrent state.
The Temporal Attention-augmented Bilinear Layer (TABL)~\cite{tran2019tabl}
learns a softmax attention over time inside a bilinear projection and
achieves results competitive with much larger networks on FI-2010 with very
few parameters. TransLOB~\cite{wallbridge2020translob} stacks dilated causal
convolutions with Transformer self-attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention},
the closest prior design to our CNN + standard-attention baseline. A
recent large-scale benchmark~\cite{prata2024lobcast} re-evaluated many of
these models under a common protocol and found that reported FI-2010 gains
often shrink when training settings, splits and metrics are unified, which
motivates our use of one shared configuration, chronological splits, and
macro-F1 in addition to accuracy.

\subsection{Efficient Attention}
Standard softmax self-attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention} forms a
$T \times T$ score matrix, so its time and memory grow as $O(T^2)$ in the
sequence length $T$. Many efficient variants have been
proposed~\cite{tay2022efficient}. Linformer~\cite{wang2020linformer}
projects keys and values to a fixed lower length; the
Performer~\cite{choromanski2021performer} approximates the softmax kernel
with random features; and the linear Transformer of Katharopoulos
\emph{et al.}~\cite{katharopoulos2020transformers} replaces the softmax
with a positive feature map $\phi$ so that attention can be computed as
$\phi(Q)\,(\phi(K)^{\top} V)$, which is $O(T)$ for fixed feature
dimension. A complementary line of work keeps exact softmax attention but
reduces memory traffic with fused GPU kernels such as
FlashAttention~\cite{dao2022flashattention}. Such kernels improve constants
but do not change the quadratic number of operations.

\subsection{Positioning of This Work}
Prior LOB models either omit attention (CNN, CNN--LSTM) or use quadratic
softmax attention (TABL, TransLOB), and they are typically evaluated on
accuracy alone. To our knowledge, linear attention has not been
systematically evaluated as a drop-in replacement for softmax attention in
a lightweight CNN-based LOB classifier. We therefore compare three models
that are identical except for the sequence-mixing block (none, softmax,
linear) and report both predictive quality and measured latency/memory as
the window length grows.
